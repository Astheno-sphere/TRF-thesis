Two instructions not applied, pending his confirmation

Query

The eHOMB date

The feedback directs replacing the placeholder in §1.1 with 2024, attributed to "the eHMGB."

Two difficulties. The body in the primary source is the eHealth Operational Management Board (eHOMB); eHMGB does not correspond to a body in the MyHealth@EU governance structure, which comprises eHMSEG and eHOMB. And the Helsedirektoratet portfolio page carrying the sentence was published around late March 2025, stating that the change proposal "ble godkjent og besluttet i eHOMB 12. mars" with no year — but the same page also reports 2026 events, so its age cannot date that sentence and the year is not recoverable from it.

The sentence is also narrower than the feedback describes: it concerns Norway's change proposal to the requirement catalogue for implementing ePrescription without eDispensation, not the adoption of cross-border architecture guidelines.

Proposed: keep the date without a year and ask whether he holds a source. An earlier version of this page proposed 12 March 2025; that inference was invalid and is withdrawn.

Query

The SPUHiN reference

The feedback proposes the title "Deliverable D5.2: Technical minimum security requirements for secure processing environments under EHDS," issued by "Norwegian Infrastructure for Health Data (SPUHiN)."

The verified title, from the Helsedirektoratet project page, is Recommendations for minimum requirements for Secure Processing Environments in Norway, produced by the SPUHiN project run jointly by Helsedirektoratet and Folkehelseinstituttet.

Proposed: remove the editorial flag as instructed, format as an APA gray-literature deliverable, retain the verified title and issuing bodies. If he holds a different version of D5.2, his citation prevails.

Accepted work

Literature-review methodology — the strongest item

Section 2.1 names databases and a date range but documents no Boolean strings, no per-search dates, no exclusion criteria, and no screening flow. In a thesis whose credibility rests on verifiability, holding every citation to a standard we do not hold our own search to is an inconsistency. Nobody else caught this.

Section 2.1 to be rewritten as a documented protocol containing every database with the date each search ran; the exact Boolean strings in a table; inclusion criteria (peer-reviewed journal and conference papers, primary regulatory and government sources, 2018–2026 for technical literature and unrestricted for primary law, English and Norwegian); exclusion criteria; a PRISMA-style screening diagram; and an explicit statement of how practitioner and gray-literature sources were treated, since those are cited as evidence of industry practice rather than as regulatory fact.

One honesty constraint. The searches ran iteratively across May and September 2026, not as a single systematic sweep. The protocol must describe what was actually done. This is a narrative review with a documented and replicable search protocol, not a systematic review, and it will be labelled as such rather than as something more impressive.

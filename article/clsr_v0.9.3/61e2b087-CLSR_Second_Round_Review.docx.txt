COMPUTER LAW & SECURITY REVIEW   /   SECOND-ROUND REVISION

Second-round review and revision notes

*Auditing After Erasure in the European Health Data Space*

Assessment: suitable for submission as a full-length legal and technical analysis once the authors approve the text and complete the declarations. The strongest evidence is the field-level conflict test. The simulation shows the consequences of the competing readings for a fixed set of events. No deployment study is needed for that claim.

## An audit question with a testable answer

The introduction, Section 3.3 and new Table 3 follow one investigation from permit to release. Two queries can produce the same aggregate while selecting different cohorts. An approval entry tells the auditor who approved the operation. An execution record may show which filter ran. Establishing why one particular patient was included requires still more evidence. The revised paper specifies how far each conclusion can reach and gives separate acceptance tests for cohort selection and output release.

## Who can handle an erasure request

Section 2.1 now treats identification as a condition for applying the individual erasure clock. It explains GDPR Article 11, the limits on access to the pseudonymisation key under EHDS Article 66(3), and the restriction on re-identification by the health data user under Article 61(3). Section 6.1 applies Article 73(2) to the independent review of downloads. This avoids suggesting that a lawful release can include identifiable patient rows.

## A public source for the Norwegian comparison

Section 2.3 and new reference [25] use the Directorate’s published privacy notice. It supports six years for listed information about healthcare professionals using the MyHealth@EU search-and-view module. The text applies 72 months as an illustrative comparison when assessing a readable payload. A broader claim about national gateway or patient-query retention would require separate evidence. The manuscript no longer depends on a private communication to support the narrower claim.

## A clearer unit of analysis

Section 5.1 now states that each draw is an access event. All 17 reused permit labels in the 100 secondary-use records have multiple assigned expiry dates. The model cannot establish how many real permits would be affected or how a patient-level request would propagate across a controller’s systems. Section 5.2 leads with the secondary-use results and gives the pooled comparison afterwards.

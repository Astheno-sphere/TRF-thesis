The thesis argues that three retention regimes collide on cross-border health audit records: the eHDSI ten-year audit floor and the Art. 73(1)(e) EHDS one-year log floor, the GDPR Art. 17 erasure right, and the Art. 68(12) EHDS six-month deletion ceiling. It formalises the collision as the TRF Model and makes the semantics of "verifiable" an explicit parameter. It shows the system is infeasible under plaintext semantics (Theorem 1) and feasible under accountability-preserving semantics (Theorem 2). It specifies Crypto-Erasure by Invalidation as the witness and demonstrates both results in a public Python sandbox over 200 synthetic records.

Source discipline, transparency and reproducibility are exemplary. The research question is sharp and the cross-sector test in §6.9a is a genuine insight. Four issues decide the verdict, and each can be fixed with work already within the candidate's reach:

The "payload" of an audit record is never pinned to a real record format. eHDSI/ATNA audit records and SPE access logs may not carry clinical content at all. If they do not, Semantics I describes a practice nobody follows (M1).
On the broad reading of Art. 68(12), the mechanism does not satisfy the ceiling. The identifiable metadata it retains is itself personal data within the SPE. The thesis's sharpest result (Corollary 1.2) and its resolution (Theorem 2) cannot both stand on the same reading (M2).
The specification and the code disagree on the salt, and the retained salt and ciphertext need a justification the text does not give (M3).
The ten-year floor, a load-bearing parameter, rests on a single secondary source. Several "finalised" claims about SPUHiN D5.2 go beyond the reference actually cited (M5).
Strengths

A well-posed, timely problem. The collision between Art. 68(12) and Art. 73(1)(e) sits in adjacent articles of one regulation. The thesis links it to the Art. 73(5) implementing-act deadline (26 March 2027), which gives it real policy relevance.
A clear conceptual move. Making "verifiable" an explicit parameter, rather than an inherited assumption, is the thesis's most transferable idea. The necessity argument built on Art. 17(3)(b) and Art. 5(1)(c) GDPR is carefully constructed.
Honest prior-art handling. The DePaul retention-and-purging programme and US Patent 12,425,209 are surfaced and distinguished on a precise ground: recoverable copies cannot satisfy a ceiling. Few MSc theses do this.
Source discipline. Every reference carries a locator. The search log is dated. The review is correctly labelled a structured narrative review, not a systematic one, and a prospective Boolean protocol is offered for replication.
Reproducibility. The thesis has a public repository, fixed seeds, recorded console runs and an exhaustive cross-check with a control case. A payload-invariance test is run rather than asserted.
The cross-sector test (§6.9a) is the best analytical result in the thesis. Politiregisterloven §17 yields zero request-free collisions. This shows the conflict depends on where the ceiling is anchored, not merely on a floor and a ceiling coexisting. The insight deserves promotion (see M4).
Rival designs (§4.10). Five alternatives are rejected for stated reasons. Rival 4 (delete and log) is handled particularly well.
Major concerns: model and mechanism

M1. The payload p(a) is not grounded in a real audit-record format

The whole model turns on splitting a record into metadata m(a) and a clinical payload p(a), defined as "the content of the clinical document exchanged" (§4.3). The thesis never shows that an audit record contains such content.

Cross-border records. The IHE ATNA audit message model (DICOM PS3.15 / RFC 3881 lineage, and FHIR AuditEvent) records participant objects: patient identifier, document identifiers, sometimes query parameters. It does not, as normally deployed, store the retrieved document. If eHDSI audit repositories do not hold document content, Semantics I ("verifiable while the payload is readable") describes no real practice. The cross-border instance of Theorem 1 is then vacuous. What an Art. 17 request actually targets is m(a), which the mechanism keeps. Rival 3 (§4.10) already concedes this point.
SPE access logs. In the code, an SPE record's payload is the string <FHIR:AuditEvent spe-access ...>, i.e. the log itself. Its metadata carries a patient pseudonym, a health-professional actor (HP-026) and an NCP (ncp=NO) (§5.6, record 100). An SPE access log records researcher activity in an environment, not a per-patient clinical exchange.
Required: define p(a) concretely for each record class, with the fields named. Ground it in the eHDSI audit specification and in the TEHDAS2 D7.4 logging requirements. Either cite evidence that audit repositories retain payload content (for example ParticipantObjectQuery or ParticipantObjectDetail values, or retained query text and outputs in an SPE), or reframe Semantics I as the semantics of a rich audit log and say how common that is. Revise the SPE record model in the code to match.

M2. On the broad reading of Art. 68(12), the mechanism does not satisfy the ceiling

Corollary 1.2 needs the broad reading: Art. 68(12) reaches all personal data "within the secure processing environment", including identifiable access logs. The thesis itself states that identifiable access logs are personal data (§1.1, §6.1). It also states that m(a) is personal data because it holds the patient pseudonym and actor identifier (§4.8, end of life).

The mechanism keeps m(a) identifiable until month 12. At tπ + 6 the SPE therefore still holds personal data, and the ceiling is breached on the very reading that creates Corollary 1.2. Theorem 2 escapes this only because C3 is formalised on payload accessibility α alone. That is, in effect, the narrow reading. The sentence in §4.8, "the environment is thereby cleared of accessible personal data at the ceiling while continuing to satisfy the floor", does not follow.

Address the obvious locational rival (a "Rival 6") in any case. If the ceiling is defined by location, exporting the access logs at permit closure to an audit store outside the SPE (held by the HDAB, for example) satisfies both provisions literally, without cryptography. The thesis must explain why that does not dissolve Corollary 1.2.
Also rewrite §6.1. It opens with "two provisions that cannot both be satisfied", which is the most contested claim, and it contradicts Theorem 2. Lead with the robust result instead.

M3. Salt and ciphertext: specification, code and justification disagree

Contradiction. §4.8 says the salt r(a) is not destroyed at invalidation, and §5.6 says "retain metadata, commitment, and salt unchanged". §4.9 lists r(a) in the residue. But trf_checker.py sets rec.salt = None  # salt destroyed with the key (Ch4 §4.8) at invalidation. Appendix A.4 and the 21 Sept change-record entry ("Salt destroyed with the key") say the same. The console output in A.13 prints salt=None.
Substance. A salt stored beside the metadata turns the commitment into a guess-confirmation oracle for low-entropy payloads. That is exactly the threat §4.9 describes. The stated reason for keeping it (verifying an opened payload "during the pre-invalidation period") does not need the salt after invalidation. The only real use afterwards is a third party, such as the treating country's system, opening the commitment against a copy it still holds. If that is the use case, argue it. Otherwise destroy the salt at invalidation, or store it under k(a).
Ciphertext. Nothing in Semantics II requires the payload ciphertext once the key is destroyed. Deleting it at invalidation removes the ten-year algorithmic-break (harvest-now-decrypt-later) risk that §4.9 calls "unavoidable". Either delete it, or justify why it is kept.
Code hygiene. self.salt = os.urandom(16) if False else bytes([...]) contains a dead branch. The salt is derived deterministically from rid, which the thesis acknowledges (A.4). Replace the line with a seeded generator or an explicit --reproducible flag.
M4. The theorems are definitional; move the weight to what is not

Theorem 1 follows in three lines from Vᴵ = α. Theorem 2 is conceded to be "close to immediate" (§4.7). The Semantics II checker returns zero violations by construction (§5.7), and its V_II function only checks that m(a), c(a) and ι(a) are present. Condition (i), "intact and unmodified", is never tested. An examiner will ask what the formal apparatus adds.

Suggested changes:

Call the results Propositions and put the argumentative weight on the necessity analysis (§4.2) that justifies Semantics II.
Promote §6.9a into Chapter 4 as a formal result. A floor and a ceiling anchored to the same event are jointly satisfiable under Semantics I whenever the ceiling is at least as long as the floor. A ceiling anchored to an external event (such as permit expiry) can cross the floor. This anchoring condition is the thesis's genuinely non-trivial structural insight, and it currently sits in an unnumbered subsection of the Discussion.
Make V_II test integrity. Chain m(a) and ι(a) entries with a hash chain (a few lines of code). Add a tamper test showing V_II fails when metadata is altered. This gives the Semantics II check real content.
Shorten Chapter 5 accordingly. The violation rates (37%, 53%, 50%) are generator artefacts, as the thesis itself says.
Major concerns: sources, evaluation and positioning

M5. Load-bearing claims need primary sources

The thesis sets its own rule that primary text prevails over secondary commentary (§2.1.5). In five places it does not yet meet that rule.

The ten-year floor (F = 120). This parameter drives the abstract, Corollary 1.1 and Table 5.1. It is sourced only to Simjanoska Misheva et al. (2025), a JMIR tutorial on AI Act compliance. Cite the eHDSI/MyHealth@EU primary document (security policy or audit requirements adopted by eHMSEG) with a section number. If it cannot be obtained, say so and present 120 months as an illustrative parameter. Corollary 2.2 makes the results F-independent, so the damage is contained, but the headline figure is not.
SPUHiN D5.2 "finalised". §1.2, §2.6, §4.11 and the Statement of Contribution say the gap "survives the final national document". The reference entry, however, is the hearing version with no stable locator. §6.8 concedes the absence is "observed in the published checklists rather than established against the full deliverable". Align every claim to what was actually inspected.
Quote Art. 68(12) and Art. 73(1)(e) verbatim. The location-versus-provenance argument (§4.6) turns on exact wording ("the data within the secure processing environment"), yet the provisions are only paraphrased. Quote the Official Journal English text. Ideally check one further language version, since CJEU interpretation compares language versions.
EHDS dates in §2.2. The claim that Member States must designate a digital health authority "by June 2025" and certify systems "by January 2026" (sourced to an EY tax alert) conflicts with the Art. 105 application dates given in the same paragraph. Verify against the Regulation and drop the law-firm source.
Non-peer-reviewed sources carrying argument. Aikebaier (2026, unreviewed ePrint), VeritasChain (a self-declared standards body), SecuPi and a OneUptime blog post are caveated in the text. Table 2.1 nevertheless gives them rows alongside peer-reviewed systems. Consider moving them to a footnote row or a separate "practitioner and preprint" band. Also check whether Feng et al. (2022, arXiv) has a peer-reviewed version.
M6. The DSR evaluation step is thin

Evaluation is analytical only, and the expert instrument (Appendix C) is designed but not executed. For a specification-and-demonstration MSc this is acceptable if framed explicitly, but the jury may see the Peffers cycle as incomplete. With about six weeks left, the options are:

Minimum (recommended): a requirement-by-requirement traceability matrix of the mechanism against TEHDAS2 D7.4 and the SPUHiN checklist. This turns the prose integration analysis of §4.11 into a checkable table.
Optional: two or three written expert walkthroughs. Check with Sikt and the HiMolde research office whether a notification is needed, rather than assuming either way; the four-to-six-week lead time makes this tight.
Supervisor's note on Appendix C. Items U1 and A1 were my suggested anchors. The candidate may reword A1 ("full confidence"), and it should be reworded. Keeping an item known to overstate the claim, "because silently correcting a supervisor's wording would misrepresent the record", is unnecessary. Record the change in one line.

M7. Fit with the programme (LOG953, Sustainable Energy Logistics)

Examiners from a logistics programme will probe the information-logistics framing. §2.7 is candid that the framing is load-bearing at one point only: the per-record floor parameter. The seafarer-medical-certificate example and the OECD data-autarky figure are tangential. §1.4 overreaches in saying the thesis "imports a solution structure that logistics has operated for decades".

Strengthen the framing with one or two concrete logistics anchors. Customs record-keeping under the Union Customs Code (a floor anchored to the declaration) against destruction orders for seized goods (an externally anchored ceiling) is one candidate worth checking against the anchoring result of §6.9a.
Say plainly in §1.5 how the thesis sits within Sustainable Energy Logistics, given that energy metrics are out of scope. One honest sentence is better than silence.
